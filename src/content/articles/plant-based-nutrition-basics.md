## Think about the whole routine

A food routine is more than a single ingredient. Rather than expecting one plant product to solve a health concern, consider the variety of foods you enjoy and how they fit into your day.

## Ask practical questions

Can you identify the ingredients? Do you have an allergy or dietary restriction? Are you relying on a claim that has a clear, credible source? These questions are useful when exploring unfamiliar foods and botanical products.

## Avoid the superfood shortcut

A marketing label is not a complete nutrition assessment. This introduction does not supply nutrient targets or disease-prevention promises. Those topics need authoritative sources and, sometimes, individual professional advice.

## Seek guidance when needed

A qualified dietitian or other appropriate healthcare professional can help with individual dietary needs, especially for children, pregnancy or a medical condition. A general article cannot replace that advice.
