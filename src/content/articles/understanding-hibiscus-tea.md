## A bright botanical infusion

The hibiscus catalogue entry refers to Hibiscus sabdariffa calyces. It does not refer to every plant commonly called hibiscus. Proper identification and food-grade sourcing matter.

## Make flavour the starting point

Hibiscus infusions are often chosen for their tart character and colour. Follow the actual product label for preparation, and check whether a blend includes other ingredients or added sugar.

## Tradition is not a treatment promise

Descriptions of traditional use should name their sources and cultural context. We are not presenting an unsourced traditional practice as evidence that a tea treats a condition.

## Reading research carefully

A study’s preparation, participant group and outcome matter. A finding about an extract is not automatically a finding about a home-brewed infusion. A detailed research summary awaits editorial verification.

## Check suitability

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, giving a product to a child or using medication, seek individual professional advice before use. Do not assume that a botanical beverage is suitable for everyone.
